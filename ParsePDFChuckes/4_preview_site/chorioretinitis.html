B.

All others

  • Treatment in previous 5 years
  • Multiple episodes
  • Likely to recur
  • Does not meet VA specifications

Submit the following for FAA review:

  • 1. A current, detailed Clinical Progress Note generated from a clinic visit with treating retinal specialist (preferred) or ophthalmologist no more than 90 days before the AME exam.

It should include a summary of the history of the condition or diagnosis; current medications, dosages, and side effects (if any); clinical exam findings; results of any testing performed; diagnosis; assessment and plan (prognosis); and follow-up.

  • 2. It must specifically include:
  • Diagnosis & Etiology: Specific cause/etiology and presence of any underlying immunodeficiency.
  • Date treatment was completed and verification that the condition is fully resolved with no active disease.
  • Prognosis and confirmation of no current visual impairment.
  • 3. Visual Acuity. Best corrected visual acuity for distant and near vision (intermediate - if age 50 or older and First or Second Class). If vision standards are not met, that should be discussed. Include if the visual acuity is expected to change.
  • 4. Visual field testing (24-2 or 30-2) performed no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam.
  • 5. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) testing performed no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam.

DEFER

Submit the information to the FAA for a possible Special Issuance.

Summarize findings in Item 60.

To determine the applicant’s eligibility for certification, the AME must review a current, detailed Clinical Progress Note generated from a clinic visit with the treating physician or specialist no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam.If the applicant meets ALL the acceptable certification criteria listed below, the AME can issue. Applicants for first- or second- class must provide this information annually; applicants for third-class must provide the information with each required exam.

AME MUST REVIEW

ACCEPTABLE CERTIFICATION CRITERIA

Treating ophthalmologist finds the condition stable on current regimen and no changes recommended.

[ ] Yes

Age at diagnosis

[ ] 40 or older

FAA Form 8500-14 or equivalent treating physician report that documents the considerations below:

[ ] Yes

Acceptable types of glaucoma

[ ] Open Angle being monitored and stable, Ocular Hypertension or Glaucoma Suspect being monitored and stable, or previous history of Narrow Angle/Angle Closure Glaucoma which has been treated with iridectomy/iridotomy (surgical or laser) and is currently stable.

NOT acceptable: Normal Tension Glaucoma, secondary glaucoma due to inflammation, trauma, or the presence of any other significant eye pathology (e.g. neovascular glaucoma due to proliferative diabetic retinopathy or an ischemic central vein occlusion or uveitic glaucoma)

Documented nerve damage or trabeculectomy (filtration surgery)

[ ] No

Medications

[ ] None or Prostaglandin analogs (Xalatan, Lumigan, Travatan or Travatan Z), Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (Trusopt and Azopt), Beta blockers (Timoptic, etc), or Alpha agonist (Alphagan). Combination eye drops are acceptable

NOT acceptable for CACI: Pilocarpine or other miotics, cycloplegics (Atropine), or oral medications.

Medication side effects

[ ] None

Intraocular pressure

[ ] 23 mm Hg or less in both eyes

ANY evidence of defect or reported Unreliable Visual Fields

Acceptable visual field tests: Humphrey 24-2 or 30-2 (either SITA or full threshold), Octopus (either TOP or full threshold). Other formal visual field testing may be acceptable but you must call for approval. Confrontation or screening visual field testing is not acceptable.

Retinal Dystrophies (Cone, Rod, Retinitis Pigmentosa)

All Classes

(Updated 03/25/2026)

DISEASE/CONDITION

EVALUATION DATA

DISPOSITION

A. Any history of

Note: In most cases, retinal dystrophies (e.g. Cone, Rod, Rod- Cone, Cone-Rod, Retinitis Pigmentosa) are incompatible with medical certification/clearance.

Submit the following for FAA review:

1. A current, detailed Clinical Progress Note* generated from a clinic visit with a board-certified Retinal Specialist (subspecialty of ophthalmology), no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam. An FAA 8500-7 or 8500-14 are NOT acceptable. The progress note must include:

  • A detailed summary of the history of the condition;
  • Current medications, dosage, and side effects (if any);
  • Physical exam findings;
  • results of any testing performed;
  • diagnosis;
  • assessment and plan;
  • prognosis; and follow-up.

2. It must specifically include:

  • The eye specialist’s interpretation of any testing performed.
  • Whether the dystrophy is associated with any medical syndrome.
  • Any treatments.

3. Copies of the most recent testing already performed for this condition such as:

  • Reliable threshold visual fields.
  • Electroretinography (ERG);
  • Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT);
  • Fundus imaging and autofluorescence;
  • Any other testing already performed.

4. Visual acuity testing no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam.

*NOTE: Submission of 8500-7 or 8500-14 instead of actual clinical progress notes is NOT acceptable.

DEFER

Submit the information to the FAA.
